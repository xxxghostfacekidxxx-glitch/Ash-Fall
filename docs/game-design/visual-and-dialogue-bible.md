# Ash-Fall: The Descent — Visual & Dialogue Bible

## Creative north star

**A colorful punk-cartoon action RPG where the world is hideous, the characters are dramatic idiots, and the loot is worth committing several fictional felonies for.**

The game borrows broad, high-level traits from irreverent alternative animation: angular silhouettes, elastic expressions, hostile little weirdos, lurid colors, claustrophobic industrial spaces, manic timing, and dark absurdist comedy. All characters, creatures, settings, dialogue, and visual designs must be original. Do not reproduce Invader Zim characters, signature designs, scripts, catchphrases, or exact compositions.

The art should be funny at a glance and unsettling on a second look. The combat and RPG systems remain substantial; comedy is seasoning, not a substitute for mechanics.

## Visual language

- **Silhouette first:** spindly limbs, huge boots, crooked shoulders, triangular noses, oversized gloves, asymmetry, and expressive negative space.
- **Linework:** thick, confident, irregular black outlines; angular shapes; deliberately wonky perspective; sharp corners broken by occasional rubbery curves.
- **Palette:** toxic lime, bruised violet, hot magenta, warning-sign yellow, rusty orange, and ink-black. Use color as a gameplay signal: enemy telegraphs and interactables must remain readable.
- **Materials:** scratched plastic, rusty sheet metal, cheap vinyl, slime, dented steel, cracked enamel, and suspiciously organic machinery.
- **Animation:** hard pose changes, squash-and-stretch, held reaction frames, abrupt snaps, tiny anticipation followed by excessive impact, and brief grotesque visual punchlines. Never sacrifice hit readability.
- **Environment design:** crooked factories, condemned malls, municipal nightmare offices, sewer cathedrals, trash moons, alien junkyards, and rooms designed by people who should not be allowed near a ruler.
- **UI:** diegetic-looking labels, warning stickers, stamped inventory tags, chunky icon silhouettes, comic impact bursts, and playful glitch accents. Menus must still be legible, keyboard-accessible, and usable on small screens.
- **Combat feedback:** bold hit flashes, readable ground warnings, dramatic knockback, exaggerated enemy reactions, and loot beams that remain distinguishable by rarity without relying on color alone.

## Dialogue voice

Dialogue should feel fast, hostile, theatrical, and slightly over-rehearsed. Characters treat tiny inconveniences as civilization-ending catastrophes. They are intensely confident about terrible ideas, deeply offended by reality, and occasionally reveal something sincere before immediately ruining the moment.

### Rules
1. **Escalate quickly:** begin with a petty complaint, then escalate to a cosmic threat, bureaucratic meltdown, or wildly disproportionate solution.
2. **Specific beats beat random noise:** use precise absurd details, not strings of unrelated nonsense.
3. **Give every speaker a rhythm:** villains grandstand; workers mutter in exhausted specifics; merchants are aggressively transactional; monsters have one bizarre fixation; the protagonist can be dry, feral, or melodramatic.
4. **Keep lines punchy in combat:** barks should usually fit in one short sentence and not obscure critical audio cues.
5. **Let silence land:** a pause, stare, or tiny reaction can be funnier than another joke.
6. **Use profanity as emphasis, not wallpaper:** it should sharpen character and timing.
7. **Punch up at institutions, vanity, greed, incompetence, and cosmic indifference.** Avoid making marginalized identities the butt of the joke.
8. **Keep the world playable:** dialogue must not bury quest instructions, item stats, warnings, or accessibility information under comedy.

## Original sample dialogue

**Quest-giver, a municipal necromancer:**  
"Three hundred unpaid parking tickets have achieved sentience. They are nesting in the courthouse vents. Please resolve this before the mayor's skeleton notices."

**Merchant, who hates customers:**  
"That sword is cursed. The curse is included in the price. No, I will not separate them. Stop touching the display."

**Boss intro, the Departmental Maw:**  
"YOU HAVE ENTERED THE APPEALS PROCESS. YOUR APPEAL HAS BEEN DENIED. YOUR APPEAL OF THE DENIAL HAS ALSO BEEN DENIED."

**Low-health combat bark:**  
"I AM NOT DYING. I'M CREATING A VERY CONVINCING MEDICAL SITUATION."

**Legendary item flavor text:**  
"THE UNLICENSED CHIROPRACTOR — It fixes your spine. It does not ask which direction."

**Loot goblin:**  
"THIS ISN'T STEALING. IT'S A MOBILE RELOCATION OF YOUR FINANCIAL MISTAKES!"

These are tone examples, not fixed catchphrases. Avoid repeating the same gag across multiple NPCs.

## Gameplay tone contract

- Preserve the Diablo-II-inspired loop: explore, fight, collect, compare, build, return stronger, and descend again.
- Every class must have distinct combat verbs, silhouettes, skill effects, and dialogue cadence.
- Loot rarity must be mechanically meaningful. Funny names cannot conceal weak item readability.
- Bosses need learnable telegraphs, distinct phases, and mechanics that reward attention.
- Random encounters should produce surprising combinations, not random difficulty spikes.
- Persistent characters and progression should be designed for server-validated saves when connected to Ash-Fall accounts.
- The visual style should support large enemy crowds without making the battlefield unreadable.

## Art and production checklist

Before approving an asset or scene, ask:
- Is its silhouette recognizable at gameplay zoom?
- Does it feel original rather than like a renamed existing character?
- Is the color contrast strong enough for combat and color-vision differences?
- Is the joke understandable quickly, without a paragraph of explanation?
- Does it support the world's punk-cartoon identity?
- Does it remain legible on mobile-sized screens if browser play is supported?

## One-sentence test

**It should look like a cartoon drawn by a furious insomniac, sound like a committee meeting in a haunted dumpster, and play like a genuinely excellent loot-driven action RPG.**
